## Requesting feedback

You can ask any colleague for feedback. They are notified, and their response is
attached to your record.

**Feedback here is attributed, not anonymous.** The person's name is on their
response, and they know that when they write it. This produces more useful,
more careful feedback than an anonymous channel — and less brutal honesty. It is
a deliberate trade.

Ask well, and you get something worth reading:

- **Ask close to the event.** Feedback on a project that ended six months ago is
  a memory test.
- **Ask a specific question.** "Any feedback?" gets "you're doing great."
  "Was my design doc clear enough to act on?" gets an answer.
- **Ask people who saw the work**, not only people who like you.

## Giving feedback

Someone asking you for feedback has invited criticism. Wasting that with "great
to work with" is a small unkindness.

- Describe what you observed, not what you concluded. "The spec changed twice
  after we started building" rather than "poor planning".
- Say what you would like to see instead. Feedback without a direction is a
  complaint.
- Write what you are willing to say to their face, because your name is on it.

## Development plans

A development plan connects a gap to something you will actually do: a
competency to work on, an action, and a date. It is visible to your manager, who
is expected to support it.

Plans work when they are small and specific. "Improve communication" is not a
plan. "Write the design doc for the payments migration and get it reviewed by
March" is one — it produces evidence either way.

## Learning resources

The learning library lists resources mapped to competencies, so the ones
suggested to you are tied to your actual gaps rather than a generic catalogue.

## Career paths

Career paths show which position typically follows yours, the typical time in
role, and the competency levels that role expects.

Read this as information, not as a promise. A path shows the requirements for
the next role; meeting them makes you a credible candidate when a position
exists. It is not a queue, and it does not create an entitlement — but it does
mean nobody has to guess what the bar is.
