This system handles **performance**: the goals you agree with your manager, the
check-ins along the way, the review at the end of the cycle, and the skills and
development that come out of it.

It runs on a server inside your own office. Your review data does not leave the
building.

## What you will actually do here

Most people open this a handful of times a year:

1. **At the start of a period** — write your goals and agree them with your
   manager.
2. **Every month or so** — post a check-in saying how each goal is going.
3. **At review time** — write your self-review, then read your manager's once it
   is released.
4. **Occasionally** — give a colleague feedback when they ask, or look at your
   competency gaps and development plan.

That is the whole product for most staff. Managers and HR have more to do; see
the sections for those roles.

## What this system does not do

It is not a complete HR system, and several things you might expect are
deliberately absent:

- **No payroll.** No salary, no payslips, no BIR, SSS, PhilHealth or Pag-IBIG.
- **No timekeeping.** No DTR, no clock-in, no overtime.
- **No leave.** Filing and balances live in whatever system you already use.
- **No 201 file.** Contracts, clearances and government numbers stay where they
  are.

If you are looking for any of those, this is the wrong system — ask HR which one
your company uses.

## Why the separation

Keeping performance data apart from payroll and statutory records is a
deliberate design decision, not an oversight. It means this system holds far
less about you than a full HR platform would, which is the point. See
**[Your privacy](#your-privacy)** for exactly what is stored.
