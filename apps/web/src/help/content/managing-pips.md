A Performance Improvement Plan is a documented, time-bound plan to close a
specific performance gap. It is a serious step and should be a small minority of
your management.

## Before starting one

A PIP is not the first conversation. If the person would be surprised by it, the
problem is that feedback was not given earlier, and a PIP now is unfair as well
as ineffective.

Ask honestly: **is this a can't or a won't?**

- *Can't* — a skills or clarity gap. A development plan, coaching or a role
  change is usually the right tool.
- *Won't* — an engagement or conduct problem. A PIP addresses performance; it is
  the wrong instrument for a conduct issue.

Also check whether the goals were achievable. Someone missing targets that were
never realistic does not have a performance problem.

## What a PIP must contain

- **The specific gap**, in observable terms — not "attitude", not "not a team
  player".
- **What success looks like**, measurably. If you cannot say what would count as
  passing, do not start.
- **What support is provided.** A PIP with obligations only on one side is not a
  plan.
- **A review date**, and interim checkpoints.

Record progress against it as you go. A PIP with no entries between opening and
closing is not evidence of anything except that a form was filled in.

## Philippine context — read this part

This system records a performance plan. **It is not a disciplinary process and
it does not satisfy legal due process.**

Under the Labour Code, terminating for just cause requires the twin-notice rule:
a written notice to explain, a genuine opportunity to be heard, and a written
notice of decision. Termination for failure to meet standards has its own
requirements, including that the standards were communicated at engagement —
which for a probationary employee means at hiring, in writing.

None of that happens in this system. **Involve HR at the start of any PIP that
could plausibly end in separation, not at the end.** A well-run PIP that skipped
the statutory process is still an illegal dismissal claim.

Probationary employees deserve particular care: the regularisation decision has
its own timeline and standards, and a PIP is not a substitute for them.

## Closing it

Close honestly. If the gap closed, say so and stop — a PIP that quietly stays
open forever is a punishment, not a plan. If it did not close, that is an HR
conversation, and it should already be in progress.
