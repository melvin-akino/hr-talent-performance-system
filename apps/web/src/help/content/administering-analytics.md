Every chart here runs under your own permissions. A manager sees their subtree
and HR sees the organisation, from identical calculations — so two people can
compare notes without either seeing something they should not.

## Rating distribution

The spread of ratings, banded against **the cycle's own scale** rather than a
fixed 1–5, so a cycle that used a different scale still reads correctly.

What to look for:

- **Everything clustered on 4** — nobody is differentiating. Common where
  managers avoid difficult conversations, and it makes the rating useless for
  every downstream decision.
- **Everything clustered on 3** — the same avoidance, wearing different clothes.
- **A department far from the rest** — either a genuinely different team or an
  uncalibrated manager. Rater comparison tells you which.

There is no correct shape to force. Mandating a curve invents underperformers to
fill a quota. The purpose is to notice when a distribution reflects the rater
rather than the rated.

## Rater comparison

How far each reviewer sits from the group average.

A consistently generous manager is not dishonest — they are uncalibrated, and
their team's ratings are worth less in any cross-team comparison. Discuss it
with them; do not silently correct it every cycle.

## The nine-box

Performance on one axis, potential on the other.

**Potential is recorded during calibration, not calculated.** Deriving it from
performance would produce a diagonal line that repeats what you already know.
Someone can be outstanding in their role and correctly low on potential for a
much larger one — that is a normal, valuable finding, not an insult.

Two warnings:

- **People with no rating or no potential are counted and reported separately,
  not dropped.** A grid that silently shrinks is how a nine-box lies. Check that
  count before you trust the picture.
- **A box is not a plan.** The grid identifies conversations to have. Treating a
  box as a label — and telling people which one they are in — reliably destroys
  its usefulness within one cycle.

## Calibration movement

What moderation actually changed. Useful for two questions: whether calibration
is doing anything at all, and whether it is consistently moving one department's
ratings in one direction.

## Trend and completion

**Trend** follows an individual across cycles. One cycle is a data point; three
is a pattern. A single bad cycle after several good ones usually says something
about the year, not the person.

**The completion funnel** shows where a cycle is stuck — generated, submitted,
calibrated, signed off, acknowledged. Use it while the cycle is running; it is
the fastest way to find the stage that is blocked.
