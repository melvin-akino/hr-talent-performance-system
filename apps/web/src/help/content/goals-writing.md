A goal in this system has a **target you can measure**, not just an intention.
That constraint is the whole point: at the end of the period, attainment is
calculated, not argued about.

## The parts of a goal

**Title** — what you are trying to achieve, in a line. "Reduce escaped defects",
not "Quality".

**Measure** — the thing being counted, and which direction is good:

- *Higher is better* — revenue, satisfaction, coverage, delivery
- *Lower is better* — defects, cost per unit, build time, response time

Getting the direction right matters more than it sounds. A cost goal marked
"higher is better" will rate the worst result as the best one.

**Baseline** — where you are starting from. Optional, but without it "reduce
defects to 5" does not say whether that is a small improvement or a heroic one.

**Target** — the number that counts as achieving it.

**Weight** — how much of your overall result this goal represents.

## Weights must total 100%

Across all your goals for the period, the weights add to 100. A goal weighted 60
matters three times as much as one weighted 20.

HR can see whose weights do not total 100, and will chase it, because attainment
is meaningless until they do. If you have two goals at 50/50, that is fine. Four
at 25 each is fine. Three at 30 is not — it leaves 10% of you unaccounted for.

## What makes a good goal here

- **Something you influence.** If you cannot move the number, it is not your
  goal.
- **A number you can actually get.** If nobody measures it monthly, you will not
  be able to check in on it honestly.
- **Few, not many.** Three to five goals is normal. Ten goals weighted 10% each
  means nothing is a priority.
- **Written for a reader in December.** You will be reading this at review time
  having forgotten the context. So will your manager.

## Getting it approved

You draft the goal, then submit it. Your manager approves it, or sends it back
with comments.

**Until it is approved, it is not counted.** A goal sitting in draft at the end
of the period contributes nothing to your attainment, no matter how much work
went into it. If your manager has not acted, chase them — the deadline does not
care whose queue it is sitting in.

## Changing a goal mid-period

Circumstances change and the system allows it, but a goal edited in November to
match what happened is not a goal, it is a report. Agree changes with your
manager when the change happens, not when the review does.
