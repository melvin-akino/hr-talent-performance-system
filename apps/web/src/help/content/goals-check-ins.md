A check-in is a short, dated statement of how a goal is going. It takes a
minute. Its purpose is to make the review boring — if you have twelve honest
check-ins, nothing in December is a surprise to anyone.

## What you record

**A status:**

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| **On track** | Current trajectory reaches the target. |
| **At risk** | It will not, unless something changes. |
| **Off track** | It will not, and it needs intervention now. |

**The current value**, where you have one — the actual number so far.

**A comment** — one or two lines on what moved and what is in the way. This is
the part your future self will thank you for.

## How often

Each goal period sets a cadence — usually monthly. Miss it and the goal shows as
overdue to you and to your manager.

## Say "at risk" early

There is a strong pull toward marking everything on track until it is obviously
not. Resist it.

- **At risk in month three** is a manageable conversation. It is when help is
  still useful.
- **Off track in month eleven** is an incident. Nobody can do anything except
  record it.

Two consecutive bad check-ins put the goal on your manager's escalation list.
That is a feature, not a punishment — it is how you get attention before the
outcome is fixed.

## Honesty is cheaper than optimism

A check-in history that says on-track, on-track, on-track, then a missed target
reads worse at review time than one that flagged risk in April and shows what
was tried. The first looks like you did not know. The second looks like you
managed it.

Your manager can see the whole check-in history when they write your review. It
is evidence, and it works in your favour far more often than against it.
