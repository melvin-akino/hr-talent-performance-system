You see everyone below you in the reporting line — your direct reports and
theirs. If someone is missing, their reporting line is wrong; ask HR.

## Approving goals

Before approving, check four things:

1. **Weights total 100%.** Anything else makes attainment meaningless, and it is
   far easier to fix now than in December.
2. **The direction is right.** A cost or defect goal must be *lower is better*.
   This is the single most common error, and it silently inverts the result.
3. **The target is real.** Can they actually get the number monthly? A goal that
   cannot be checked in on will not be.
4. **It is theirs to move.** If the outcome depends entirely on another team, it
   is not a goal for this person.

**An unapproved goal does not count.** If it is still in draft when the period
ends, it contributes nothing — regardless of the work done. Clearing your
approval queue is not administrative tidiness; it is the difference between
someone's effort counting and not.

## Monitoring, and what it is actually for

The Monitoring screen shows check-in compliance, overdue goals, and escalations
— goals with two consecutive bad check-ins.

Use it monthly. Its entire value is that it surfaces problems while they are
still solvable. A goal that goes off track in April and is discovered in
November has cost you the year.

Two patterns worth reacting to:

- **Someone with no check-ins at all.** Usually means they have disengaged from
  the process, or their goals were never realistic. Both are worth a
  conversation, and neither improves on its own.
- **Everything permanently on track, then a miss.** Their check-ins are not
  honest, which means you have no early warning on anyone in that team.

## Making it safe to say "at risk"

You get the early warning only if people believe flagging risk is safe. If the
first at-risk check-in is met with irritation, you will get on-track check-ins
until the outcome is unavoidable — and you will find out last.

The most useful thing you can do here costs nothing: respond to an early at-risk
flag with help rather than displeasure, once, visibly. Everyone on the team
learns from that.
