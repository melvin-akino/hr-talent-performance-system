## How a cycle runs

A review cycle moves through four stages, in order:

1. **Self** — you assess yourself.
2. **Supervisor** — your manager assesses you.
3. **Calibration** — HR and managers compare ratings across the organisation.
4. **Sign-off** — the rating is finalised and released to you.

You participate in stage 1 and read the result after stage 4. Stages 2 and 3
happen without you, which is normal and not a sign that something is being
hidden — see *Calibration* below.

## Writing your self-review

The form is set by HR and usually includes commentary on your goal results, an
overall rating, strengths, and development areas.

Your goal results are shown above the form. **You do not need to restate the
numbers** — your manager can see them. Use the space to explain what the numbers
do not: what changed during the year, what you did about it, what you would do
differently.

Two habits that make a self-review useful:

- **Be specific.** "Improved release quality" is unverifiable. "Cut escaped
  defects from 12 to 5 by adding contract tests in March" is not.
- **Do not undersell, do not oversell.** Your manager has the same data you do.
  A self-review that disagrees with the evidence in either direction costs you
  credibility.

Once submitted, a self-review cannot be edited. Write it in one sitting when you
have the time.

## Calibration, and why your rating can change

After managers write their reviews, ratings are compared across teams. This
exists because managers are not calibrated to each other: one rates generously,
another strictly, and without a correction your rating would depend partly on
who your manager happens to be.

Your rating can move up or down at this stage. If it moves, your manager should
explain why. Asking is reasonable.

## What the rating means

The default scale runs 1 to 5:

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 — Does not meet** | Performance is below what the role requires. |
| **2 — Partially meets** | Some expectations met, gaps in others. |
| **3 — Meets** | The role was done well. This is a good rating. |
| **4 — Exceeds** | Consistently beyond what the role requires. |
| **5 — Outstanding** | Exceptional and rare. |

**3 is not a bad result.** It means you did the job you were hired to do. In a
functioning distribution most people are a 3, and a company where everyone is a
4 has stopped measuring anything.

Your company may use a different scale; the one on your form is authoritative.

## After it is released

You will be asked to **acknowledge** it. Acknowledging records that you have
seen it — it is not agreement, and it does not waive anything.

If you disagree, acknowledge it and raise the disagreement with your manager or
HR. Refusing to acknowledge does not change the rating; it just leaves HR
chasing you.

A signed-off review is final. It becomes part of the record used for
development, promotion discussions and, over time, your trend across cycles.
