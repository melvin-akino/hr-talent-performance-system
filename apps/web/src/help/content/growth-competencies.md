Goals measure **what** you delivered. Competencies describe **how** you work —
judgement, communication, ownership, mentoring, and whatever else your company's
framework defines.

## Levels are behaviours, not scores

Each competency has levels, and each level has a written behavioural indicator.
They describe scope, not effort. For example, for technical judgement:

| Level | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| 1 | Follows established patterns with guidance |
| 2 | Makes sound choices within a component |
| 3 | Weighs trade-offs across a system |
| 4 | Sets technical direction for a team |
| 5 | Shapes direction across the organisation |

Level 2 is not "trying harder than level 1". It is a different scope of work.
Your company's exact wording is on the Competencies screen.

## Required levels come from your position

Each position maps to a required level per competency. A Senior Software
Engineer is expected at a higher level than a Software Engineer — that
difference is what the seniority means.

So your required levels change when your position changes, not when you improve.

## Reading your gaps

Your gap report shows three things, and they are genuinely different:

- **Met** — assessed at or above the level your position requires.
- **Below** — assessed under it. This is where development effort pays.
- **Not assessed** — nobody has evaluated this yet.

**Not assessed is not a bad score.** It is an absence of information. If most of
your competencies are unassessed, that is a conversation to have with your
manager, not a reflection on you.

## What gaps are for

A gap is an input to your development plan, not a verdict. Two useful ways to
read the report:

- **Below on a competency your current role requires** — this is the highest-
  value thing to work on. It affects your job today.
- **Below on a competency the *next* role requires** — this is your promotion
  path. See **Career paths** on the Development screen.

Competency assessments and performance ratings are separate. Someone can hit
every goal and still be below level on mentoring; someone can be strong across
competencies and miss targets because of circumstances. Both are true at once,
and the system keeps them apart on purpose.
