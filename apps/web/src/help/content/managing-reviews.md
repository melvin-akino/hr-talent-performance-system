## Before you write

Open the person's goal results and check-in history first. The review should be
consistent with a year of evidence you both already saw. A rating that surprises
someone who has been checking in monthly means the check-ins were not being
read.

## Writing the review

**Rate against the role, not against the team.** The question is whether this
person did the job their position requires — not how they compare to the
strongest person you manage.

**Use the whole scale.** If everyone on your team is a 4, you have not rated
anyone; you have opted out. Calibration will correct it, and your ratings will
carry less weight next cycle.

**Justify with specifics.** "Consistently strong" survives no scrutiny.
"Delivered the payments migration a month early after re-scoping in March"
survives all of it. In calibration you will be asked to defend a rating, and
specifics are the only thing that works.

**Separate the year from the person.** Someone whose goals were wrecked by a
cancelled project did not necessarily perform badly. Say what happened.

## Calibration

Ratings are compared across the organisation. The purpose is to remove the part
of a rating that reflects *who the manager is* rather than how the person
performed.

You will see:

- **The distribution** across departments.
- **Rater comparison** — how far your average sits from everyone else's. If you
  are consistently above, you are the generous manager in the room, and your 4
  means less than someone else's.
- **Calibration movement** — what actually changed.

Ratings can move up or down. When one of yours moves, **you deliver that news**,
not HR. Telling someone "HR changed it" is both an abdication and, usually, a
misrepresentation of a discussion you took part in.

## Potential is not performance

During calibration you also record **potential** — the assessment of whether
someone could take on a substantially larger role, in three bands.

This is deliberately separate from performance, and it is not a reward for a
good year. A person can be outstanding in their role and correctly assessed as
low potential for a bigger one; that is not an insult, and treating it as a
demotion is how the nine-box becomes useless.

Potential is recorded rather than calculated, because inferring it from
performance would just draw a diagonal line and tell you nothing you did not
already know.

## Sign-off is final

Once HR signs off, the rating is frozen and released to the employee. There is
no undo. Raise every disagreement before sign-off, not after.
