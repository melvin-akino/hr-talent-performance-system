A Performance Improvement Plan is a written plan, agreed between you and your
reporting officer, to close a specific gap in your performance. It names the
area of concern, what needs to improve, what you will do, what your officer will
do to support you, and when it will be reviewed.

It is a serious document and it is meant to be a two-way one. The form itself says
the plan "should be a collaboration between employee and reporting officer;
supported by their team", and that the targets and action plan "should be
acknowledged by both parties".

## What is in it

- **The area of concern** — where expectations have not been met.
- **Your target score** and the dates of the **initial** and **final** review.
- **The actions to be taken, and by when** — each step with its date.
- **Your officer's responsibility** — the support, training or resources they
  will provide. A plan with obligations on only one side is not a plan.
- **The expected outcome** if the plan is achieved, and if it is not.

You can read all of it here. You are the one person who can always see your own
plan.

## Before you sign

The form is direct about this: once acknowledged, the plan may be used as a
measure in your next performance evaluation, "thus it is imperative that all
clarifications are made and answered satisfactorily prior to signing".

So if anything is unclear — a target you do not think is realistic, a step you do
not understand, support you were told about and cannot see written down — **raise
it before you sign**, and ask for the plan to be changed if it should be.

:::warning
The acknowledgement says you are informed of the expectations, and of what may be
imposed if the targets are not met. Read it as written. If you are unsure what it
means for you, ask HCM before signing — that is what they are there for.
:::

## Signing

The plan opens for signature once your reporting officer activates it. You will be
told. Your line is the **employee** line; the other three — the reporting officer,
the department manager or area head, and an HCM representative — are signed by
those people, and you can see who has signed and when.

You can sign your line once, and a signature cannot be edited or removed
afterwards.

:::note
The form asks you to acknowledge that you are informed of the expectations. This
system records that you did, and when. It does not decide what that means in any
dispute — that is a matter for HCM and, where it comes to it, the law — so a
question raised *before* you sign is far easier to act on than one raised after.
:::

## During the plan

Your officer will review progress at the dates in the plan, and the initial and
final reviews each record a score. You can read each review as it is recorded,
including any comment of yours that was written down.
